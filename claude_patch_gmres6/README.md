# Star vs single-column patch with GMRES(6) smoothing: parameter and MPI robustness (2026-11-16)

**Question.** The multigrid smoother of the shifted auxiliary-operator preconditioner can use the star patch
(`firedrake.ASMStarPC`, construct_dim 0: the two columns of cells around a base vertex) or the smaller single-column
patch (`firedrake.ASMVankaPC`, construct_dim 1: one column of cells with the horizontal-velocity unknowns on its two
vertical facets). Is the single column as robust as the star over the paper's parameter sweeps when the level smoother
is GMRES(6), which is the solver the paper's Section 5.1 text describes? And do the iteration counts stay the same when
the problem is distributed over different numbers of MPI processes?

All paths below are relative to `yr2/` (the folder that holds `Boussinesq_implicit/` and `data/`). Everything was made
by the scripts in `Boussinesq_implicit/claude_patch_gmres6/`; the figures and the per-folder READMEs are regenerated by
`python analyse.py` (Firedrake venv python) from the raw data in `data/patch_gmres6/`. How the runs were made, what is
the same as and what differs from the earlier robustness tests, and where every result is: `PROCEDURE.md` (next to this
file).

**Follow-up of 2026-11-18 (Finding 6).** Findings 1-5 use the nonlinear preconditioner as it was on 2026-11-16, without
the pressure back-substitution (now reached with `--no_backsub`). Since commit 6c4b0cf (2026-11-17) the back-substitution
is the default of `Nonlinear_Boussinesq_Irk_SC.py`. The four nonlinear sweeps were rerun with it for both patches, and
every point with K < 1 was repeated twice with a deterministic coarse solver (`figures/70_backsub_nonlinear/`).

## Solver (both codes, both patches)

Shifted auxiliary-operator preconditioner (shift delta = C1/dt, the paper's design) + pressure elimination + Schur
fieldsplit (exact pressure-mass solve) + ONE full-multigrid cycle on the velocity-buoyancy block (3 levels, horizontal
coarsening, LU coarse solve: MUMPS on 2 or more ranks) + level smoother GMRES(6) preconditioned by additive Schwarz on
column patches (LU with RCM on each patch); outer FGMRES with right preconditioning (true residual, restart 30), f = 0,
2 MPI ranks.

| code | command (what `Bash_Generator/bash_paper_patch.py` prints) | stopping test |
|---|---|---|
| linear | `LB_time_slice.py --richardson --reordering --rtol 1e-8 --maxit 100 --mg_smoother=gmres --patch <p>` | FGMRES rtol 1e-8, at most 100 iterations |
| nonlinear | `Nonlinear_Boussinesq_Irk_SC.py --no_rotation -firedrake_1_snes_ksp_ew -firedrake_1_snes_ksp_ew_rtol0 1e-2 -firedrake_1_snes_atol 1e-09 --maxit 100 --patch <p>` | Newton with Eisenstat-Walker (PETSc v2, rtol_0 = 1e-2): EW sets the FGMRES tolerance of every Newton step (the script's --rtol 1e-6 is overridden); atol 1e-9; at most 100 FGMRES iterations per step |

- `--mg_smoother` is a new, default-preserving option of `Boussinesq_implicit/LB_time_slice.py` (added for this study).
  Without it, `--richardson` still gives what all earlier linear runs used: Chebyshev(6) smoothing and outer GMRES with
  left preconditioning. Checked with `ksp_view`, and by a verifier over all 854 runs (no PETSc option was left unused):
  `--richardson --mg_smoother gmres` gives outer fgmres (right, unpreconditioned norm), fieldsplit_1 preonly,
  mg_levels_1/2 gmres with max_it 6, ASMStarPC / ASMVankaPC, coarse LU (mumps).
- The nonlinear script's default solver already is this configuration (since commit 82cd238, 2026-07-08); it is unchanged
  since the 2026-10-16 star/single-column runs (`data/paper_robustness/data_nonlinear_*_deg2_ew_{star,vanka}`), which are
  therefore a repeat of the new nonlinear runs.

## Findings

Counts: linear = outer FGMRES iterations; nonlinear = total FGMRES iterations of the monitored time step (all Newton
steps). Ratio = single column / star at the same point. K = delta / delta_thr(dt) from `claude_paper_shift.py`: K < 1 is
the regime where the grad-div term dt/delta dominates. In these sweeps it occurs at large dt and small C1 (K does not
depend on Lz for dt <= 1000 s, the weakly stratified branch). rho1 = m Lz^2/(pi^2 dx_fine^2) is the kernel parameter of
the 2026-09-29 column-patch study.

**1. Linear code: the single column is as robust as the star.** (`11`-`14`, 207 points per patch)
- Both patches fail (reach maxit 100) at exactly the same points, all in the large-shift corner (K >= 1.8e5, dt <= 50 s):
  in the dt sweep C1 = 1 at dt <= 50 s, 0.1 at dt <= 10 s, 0.01 at dt <= 5 s, 1e-3 at dt = 1 s; in the other sweeps
  (C1 = 1e-3) dt = 1 s for Lz >= 4 km, Nx >= 20 and Nz >= 20, and Lz = 12 km at dt = 5 s. This is a property of the shift,
  not of the patch. (At Nx = 10, dt = 1 s the single column meets rtol at exactly iteration 100, against 94 for the star.)
- Where both converge (185 points): single column minus star = median 0, maximum 9 iterations; within one iteration of the
  star at 152 of 185 points; ratio median 1.00 in every sweep (maximum 1.33 dx, 1.67 dz, 3.5 dt, 5.5 ar).
- The larger excesses sit where the star needs only 2-3 iterations: small shifts in the dt sweep (C1 <= 1e-4 at
  dt >= 500 s: 2-3 against 5-7) and thin domains at large dt in the ar sweep (Lz <= 1 km, dt >= 500 s: 2-3 against 5-11;
  worst Lz = 0.25 km, dt = 1000 s: 2 against 11, and 11-14 over the ten runs of this command, see Finding 4). The
  thin-domain excess is not a K < 1 effect (K = 4.6-18 there); it
  follows a small rho1 (about 0.003-0.05), as the 2026-09-29 study's constant C(rho1) predicts. Smaller excesses (+2) also
  occur at Lz = 2 km (dt >= 1000 s), Nz >= 40 (dt >= 5000 s) and dt = 100 s for Lz <= 0.5 km or C1 = 1e-5; the only
  excess at a large count is Nz = 10, dt = 1 s (76 against 79).
- At dt = 50 s (the dt of the paper's linear history figures) the counts are identical except 2 vs 3 for Lz <= 0.5 km
  and 3 vs 4 for C1 = 1e-5.
- Against the earlier Chebyshev(6) / GMRES-left runs (`30_smoother_linear/`; smoother, Krylov method, preconditioning side
  and residual norm all change at once, so the effects are not separated): at small and moderate K the new configuration
  needs fewer outer iterations (new/old median 0.75-0.83 for the star, 0.83-0.89 for the single column); in the
  large-shift corner it fails within 100 iterations at 6 (star) / 7 (single column) points that the old configuration
  solved in 60-96 iterations, and never the other way round. The single column / star median is 1.00 under both.

**2. Nonlinear code: the single column converges wherever the star does; for K < 1 it does not gain from the smaller
shift as the star does, and its count grows with Nz.** (`21`-`24`, 220 points per patch)
- Identical failure sets: the 13 maxit points are all in the large-shift corner (K >= 455): in the dt sweep C1 = 1e-3 at
  dt <= 100 s, 5e-4 at dt <= 50 s, 1e-4 and 5e-5 at dt = 1 s; in the ar sweep Lz = 8 and 12 km at dt = 1 s. Newton steps
  are equal at 165 of the 207 converged points (single column +1 at 36, -1 at 6); the single column needs an extra Newton
  step at 28 of the 106 points with K < 1 against 8 of the 101 with K >= 1.
- By K (single column / star; extra iterations): K >= 10: median 1.00, max 2.14; extra median 0.
  1 <= K < 10: median 1.36, max 2.0; extra median 5. 0.1 <= K < 1: median 2.6, max 4.1; extra median 16.
  K < 0.1: median 2.4, max 9.5; extra median 13, max 68. (Pooled over sweeps, so the nominal point counts up to 4 times.)
- The ratio grows for K < 1 because the star improves while the single column does not: on the nominal mesh at
  C1 = 1e-5 the single column needs 19-26 for every dt from 100 s to 5e4 s (K = 4.6 down to 8e-4) while the star falls
  from 14 to 8-10. At dt = 500 s across the C1 sweep the star needs 28, 27, 19, 12, 10, 9, 7 (C1 = 1e-3 ... 1e-6), the
  single column 29, 28, 23, 24, 26, 25, 29. Over all 106 converged points with K < 1 the star needs 7-16 (8-12 outside
  the dt sweep), the single column 13-76 (median 23 outside the dt sweep).
- At dt = 50-100 s (K = 4.6-18; the paper's figs. 8, 9 and 10 left) the patches are close: dx and dz sweeps at dt = 50 s:
  star 22-25, single column 24-27; ar sweep at dt = 100 s: 10-20 against 18-22. In the paper's dt = 500 s and 5000 s
  panels (figs. 11 and 10 right, K <= 0.18) the single column needs 1.6-4.1 times the star's count (ar sweep at
  dt = 5000 s: star 10 for every Lz, single column 16, 21, 26, 24, 23 for Lz = 12, 8, 4, 2, 1 km).
- Mesh dependence at K < 1 (medians over dt >= 500 s, reproducible: the 2026-10-16 runs give the same within 2): the
  single column RISES under vertical refinement (19, 24, 28, 34, 38 for Nz = 10, 20, 40, 60, 80) and FALLS under
  horizontal refinement (25, 26, 23, 18, 18 for Nx = 10 ... 80, consistent with rho1 growing with Nx); it is lowest in the
  thickest domain (16 at Lz = 12 km against 21-26 for Lz <= 8 km). The star needs 8-12 throughout. Single extreme values
  (76 and 70 at Nz = 60, 80, dt = 5e4 s; 37 at Nx = 40, 60, dt = 500 s) are not reproducible (Finding 3).

**3. The single column's slow counts are not reproducible; the star's are.** (`40_repeatability_nonlinear/`)
- The 440 new nonlinear commands are byte-identical to the 2026-10-16 ones (same code and libraries). At the 207 points
  where both runs converged the star reproduced every count and every per-Newton-step split; the single column
  reproduced 176 totals (85 %). The 31 differing totals all have K <= 0.183 and differ by up to 33 iterations (dz,
  Nz = 60, dt = 5e4 s: 43 then 76; Nz = 80: 47 then 70); two more single-column points (one at K = 0.46) have the same
  total but a different per-step split. Near the cap the star is not fully reproducible either: at C1 = 1e-3, dt = 1 s
  the 2026-10-16 run stopped with a ConvergenceError after 10 Newton steps, the new one ended with 9 of 10 steps at maxit.
- Within this campaign the nominal configuration is run in all four sweeps with identical solver commands (only the
  output naming flag differs): the star gives identical counts in all four at every dt; the single column 26/28/26/26 at
  dt = 500 s and 23/23/26/24 at dt = 5e4 s. The worst point (Nz = 60, dt = 5e4 s) has three realisations: 43 (2026-10-16),
  48 (the new run's warm-up pass) and 76 (its monitored pass), against the star's 8, i.e. a ratio of about 5-10.
- Cause: the parallel MUMPS coarse LU. Both patches show rounding-level differences in every run with Nx >= 20 (none at
  Nx = 10), even between the warm-up and the monitored pass inside one run. A direct check (`60_mpi/README.md`, "Noise
  check"; jobs from `make_noise_jobs.py`) repeated two single-column points with K < 1 three times each: serial runs
  (PETSc's own LU on the coarse level) are bitwise identical (22, 22, 22 and 31, 31, 31), so are 2-rank runs with
  SuperLU_DIST instead of MUMPS on the coarse level (23, 23, 23 and 33, 33, 33), while 2-rank runs with MUMPS are not
  (24, 27, 24 and 43, 34, 31). With a deterministic coarse solver what remains is a small, reproducible dependence on the
  partition (22 vs 23, 31 vs 33). The star's counts are insensitive to the MUMPS noise; the single column's slowly
  converging solves at small K turn it into count changes. The extreme counts also include FGMRES(30) restarts with stagnation (in
  the 76-iteration run, Newton step 2 takes 72 iterations and the residual jumps 30 and 60 iterations into the step).
  The earlier study found far fewer within-run count changes with the pressure back-substitution fix (2 of 70 runs
  against 17 of 70, K = 1 ar sweep), and the 2026-09-29 study found no Nz growth in the velocity-buoyancy multigrid
  cycle alone (16-17 iterations for Nz = 10-80).

**4. MPI ranks: the number of ranks does not change the counts beyond the run-to-run noise of Finding 3; the only
failure is in the coarse direct solver.** (`60_mpi/`; 9 points per code with both patches on np = 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16
ranks (np <= Nx, so the Nx = 10 point stops at np = 8), three runs at np = 2 and at np = 8: 320 runs)
- The star gives the same count at every np and in every repeat at all 9 points of both codes; the linear dt = 1 s point
  (P2) reaches maxit at every np for both patches, as in the sweeps. The single column also gives one count at every np
  at all points with dt <= 50 s and at the linear dt = 1e4 s point: 8 of 9 linear and 7 of 9 nonlinear points (for
  nonlinear P6 at np = 16 see the last item).
- Its counts vary at the three points where it needs far more iterations than the star: linear P9 (Lz = 0.25 km,
  dt = 1000 s: 12 at np = 1, 12-14 at np = 2-16, against the star's 2; 11 in the main sweep), nonlinear P3 (dt = 5e4 s,
  C1 = 1e-5, K < 1: 22-27 against 8) and nonlinear P9 (Nz = 80, dt = 5000 s, K < 1: 30-49 against 10). At each of
  them the spread over all np (2, 5 and 19 iterations) is no larger than the spread between repeats at one fixed np
  (2, 3 and 19) plus 2, so changing the number of ranks does not move the count more than repeating the run does.
- That variation is the MUMPS noise of Finding 3. With a deterministic coarse solver the repeats are bitwise identical
  (nonlinear P3: 22, 22, 22 at np = 1 with PETSc's LU and 23, 23, 23 at np = 2 with SuperLU_DIST; P9: 31, 31, 31 and
  33, 33, 33), so the partition itself changes these counts by 1-2 iterations.
- Two runs aborted: the nonlinear code at Nx = 80 (P6) on 16 ranks, with both patches. The MUMPS LU factorisation of the
  multigrid coarse level failed (PETSc: FACTOR_OUTMEMORY, i.e. MUMPS ran out of the working space it had reserved; the
  machine had more than 30 GB of free memory). The coarse solve then returned NaN/Inf, the outer FGMRES stopped at
  iteration 0, and the script's `ksp_converged_rate` diagnostic aborted the run with a LAPACK error. Reruns with
  SuperLU_DIST on the coarse level, or with MUMPS and
  `-firedrake_1_shiftedschurpc_fieldsplit_1_mg_coarse_mat_mumps_icntl_14 200` (200 % extra working space instead of
  MUMPS's 20 %), converge with 22 (star) and 24 (single column) iterations and 3 Newton steps, exactly as at np = 1-8.
  The linear code at the same point, and every other np = 16 run, worked with the default settings. This is a
  coarse-solver setting, not a property of either patch or of the column smoother.
- Run times are recorded (`60_mpi/mpi_counts.csv`) but not interpreted: the machine was shared by 24-32 ranks.

**5. Bottom line for the choice of patch.** With GMRES(6) smoothing the single column is a safe replacement for the star
in the linear code: it converges at exactly the same points and needs at most a few extra iterations where both need
very few. In the nonlinear code with the preconditioner as committed it also converges wherever the star does and costs
about the same at K >= 1 (dt = 50-100 s at C1 = 1e-5), but for K < 1 it does not profit from the smaller shift (about
20-30 iterations where the star needs 7-16), it grows under vertical refinement, and its counts there vary from run to
run, while the star's are stable. With the pressure back-substitution (Finding 6) this penalty shrinks to about 5
iterations (single column about 9-11, star 3-6), the Nz growth mostly goes, both patches need about half as many
iterations everywhere, and the failure set shrinks from 13 to 4 points (the same for both patches). Neither patch depends on the number of MPI ranks (1-16) beyond
the noise of the parallel coarse LU; on many ranks the MUMPS coarse solve may need more working space (Finding 4).

**6. Pressure back-substitution (2026-11-18): it removes most of the single column's penalty at K < 1 and halves the
counts of both patches; the single column still needs about 2-3 times the star's (small) count there.**
(`70_backsub_nonlinear/`; 220 points per patch, same commands as Findings 2-3 except that the preconditioner applies
A_delta^-1 instead of T^-1 A_delta^-1; MUMPS coarse LU, 2 ranks; plus 2 x 106 SuperLU_DIST runs per patch at K < 1.)
- Counts, medians (ranges) of total FGMRES iterations, pooled over the four sweeps, both patches converged:

  | K | points | star, before -> with back-sub. | single column, before -> with back-sub. | ratio median, before -> with |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | K >= 10 | 78 / 87 | 30 (8-133) -> 15 (4-169) | 30 (8-131) -> 15 (5-169) | 1.00 -> 1.00 |
  | 1 <= K < 10 | 23 | 14 (10-27) -> 8 (5-13) | 21 (17-28) -> 9 (7-13) | 1.36 -> 1.12 |
  | 0.1 <= K < 1 | 21 | 10 (8-16) -> 6 (3-8) | 26 (14-38) -> 11 (8-14) | 2.60 -> 1.83 |
  | K < 0.1 | 85 | 10 (7-14) -> 3 (3-7) | 22 (13-76) -> 9 (6-14) | 2.40 -> 2.67 |

  At K < 1 the single column's excess over the star falls from a median of 13-16 iterations (max 68) to 5-6 (max 11).
  The ratio stays at about 2-3 only because the star drops to 3 iterations (one or two per Newton step): the single
  column now does profit from the small shift (about 9-11 iterations against 20-30 before), it just profits less than
  the star. At K >= 10 the two patches are still identical (ratio 1.00 at the median).
- Vertical refinement (medians over dt >= 500 s): single column 19, 24, 28, 34, 38 -> 8, 10, 11, 12, 12 for Nz = 10,
  20, 40, 60, 80 (star 10 -> 3-4 throughout). The growth falls from a factor 2 to 1.5 and levels off from Nz = 40. The
  Nx and Lz members of the single column are 7-12 (before 16-26); the star is 3-4.
- Failures (no convergence within the cap): 13 points before, 4 with the back-substitution, the same 4 for both
  patches: the dt sweep at C1 = 5e-4 (dt = 1 s) and C1 = 1e-3 (dt = 1, 10, 50 s), all with K = 2e3-5e6, i.e. the
  over-shifted end of the sweep, not a patch effect. The 9 points that the back-substitution rescues have K = 456-5e5; they
  need 57-169 iterations (dt sweep C1 >= 5e-5 at small dt; ar-sweep members 8000 and 12000 at dt = 1 s), nearly the same
  for both patches (largest difference 57 against 91 at C1 = 5e-4, dt = 50 s).
- Newton steps: median 2 (star) and 3 (single column), range 2-3 for both (before: median 3 for both).
- Reproducibility: with SuperLU_DIST on the coarse level the two repeats are bitwise identical at all 212 point-pairs.
  SuperLU_DIST and MUMPS give the same counts at all 106 star points and at 102 of the 106 single-column points (the
  other 4 differ by at most 2). The run-to-run noise of Finding 3 (single column at K < 1) is therefore small once the
  back-substitution is in; the SuperLU_DIST counts (`counts.csv`, columns `slu1_*`) are the reproducible numbers to
  quote.
- The paper's nonlinear figures redrawn from these runs: `70_backsub_nonlinear/paper_style/`.

## Caveats

- **Stopping tests.** The new linear runs stop on the true residual (FGMRES right, rtol 1e-8). The paper's current linear
  figures and the supplement's linear star/single-column comparison (SM2) were made with Chebyshev(6) and GMRES-left
  (preconditioned residual), so linear counts here are not directly comparable with them.
- **Reference solution of the error histories.** x* (linear) and u* (nonlinear) are the results of an identical warm-up
  solve in the same run (same iterative solver and tolerance), not of a direct solve. The final drop of a linear history
  to about 1e-15 is therefore an artefact; the penultimate value measures the last update. The paper's wording
  "direct-solver reference solution" (ex_article.tex around line 1804) does not match the code.
- **Nondeterminism.** See Findings 3 and 4: quote single-column counts at small K (and at the thinnest linear domain at
  large dt) as ranges or medians, not single values.
- **Known defect of the nonlinear preconditioner.** The earlier study `Boussinesq_implicit/docs/claude_studies/claude_small_patch_robustness.md`
  (Section 4.4; also `claude_notes/aux_pc_back_substitution.md`) found that the committed nonlinear auxiliary-operator
  preconditioner omits a pressure back-substitution, so it applies T^-1 A_delta^-1 instead of A_delta^-1. All nonlinear
  counts here include that defect (the configuration asked for). The only data with the fix is the K = 1 aspect-ratio
  sweep without Eisenstat-Walker (`data/claude_paper/data_claude_paper_nonlinear_ar_patch{star,vanka}[_pcfix]`, GMRES(6),
  2 ranks). There the fix halves both patches' counts (median star 15 -> 8, single column 28 -> 13); it lowers the
  single column / star ratio at dt = 500-5000 s (median 2.36 -> 1.88, 1.94 -> 1.44, 1.30 -> 1.14) but raises it at
  dt <= 100 s (e.g. 2.50 -> 3.33 at dt = 50 s). The paper's sweeps with the fix (now the script's default; Findings 1-5
  correspond to `--no_backsub`) are Finding 6.
- **K between the codes.** K uses the same formula for both codes; the earlier study (end of its Section 4.4) derives,
  without measuring it, that the nonlinear script's delta is worth 2 delta of the linear code (K_eff = 2K).
- **Cost.** Not measured here. The earlier idle-machine measurement (`Boussinesq_implicit/claude_column_patch/timing/`;
  nonlinear code, nominal mesh, one configuration, 20-27 % timing noise between repeats) found one outer iteration with
  the single column about 30 % cheaper than with the star, so the single column only wins where its count is below about

## For the writing agent: what changes against the paper

- Solvers behind the paper's figures (git history of Boussinesq_implicit): linear figs. 4-7, Tables 3-4 and SM2-linear:
  Chebyshev(6) + GMRES-left (`--richardson`); nonlinear figs. 8-12 (data of 2026-05-20, commit 7771540): Chebyshev(6) +
  GMRES-right + Eisenstat-Walker; SM2-nonlinear (2026-10-16) and this study: GMRES(6) + FGMRES-right. The paper's text
  (Section 5.1) describes GMRES(6) + FGMRES; its TODO near line 1830 and the correction list near line 2517 state the
  linear smoother the wrong way round.
- The nonlinear equations also changed after the paper's runs: the advection term in `utils.Nonlinear_velocity_Irk`
  was corrected from `div(outer(u, w))` to `div(outer(w, u))` in commit 2cd7d82 (2026-08-17). The paper's figs. 8-12
  were computed with the old form; the 2026-10-16 runs and this study use the corrected one. Nonlinear counts of the
  paper and of `50_paper_style/` therefore differ for this reason as well as for the solver (details: `PROCEDURE.md`).
- SM2 numbers from the earlier data against these GMRES(6) runs: linear "within one iteration in 165 of 191 runs" ->
  152 of 185; "at most 7 against 4 for C1 = 1e-5" -> 7 against 2 (and 6 against 2 at C1 = 1e-4); thin domains "11
  against 4" -> 11 against 2 (the star improved from 4 to 2, the single column stayed at 11; 11-14 over ten runs of
  the same command, Finding 4); nonlinear median ratio 2.6
  (K < 1) unchanged, maximum 5.9 -> 9.5 in a single run (5-10 over realisations); Nz medians 19 and 38 against the star's
  about 10, unchanged; the failure sets are the same for both patches in both data sets.
- Prefer medians and ranges over maxima for the single column at K < 1.
- The nonlinear counts of Findings 2-3 and of `50_paper_style/` are without the pressure back-substitution (now
  `--no_backsub`); the paper's nonlinear figures (commit 7771540) were also made without it. With it (Finding 6,
